# Product: Ghost Credit

## Who it is for
Nigerian small business owners who get paid by bank transfer: spas, salons, printing shops, boutiques, phone shops, food vendors. Most are busy, on their phone, and not technical.

## The one job
Before the owner releases goods or starts a service, tell them whether a "credit alert" (an SMS or a transfer screenshot) is backed by real money.

## Who sells it
Ghost Credit is a product of LGND Studio Services. Each shop gets its own setup, its own Paystack keys and its own data. Nothing is shared between shops.

## Verdicts (the only four allowed)
| Code | Shown to owner as | When |
|---|---|---|
| CONFIRMED | ALIVE | A matching payment is found in the merchant's own Paystack account |
| NOT_FOUND | GHOST | The merchant collects through Paystack, and no matching payment exists |
| SUSPICIOUS | SUSPICIOUS | Only the alert itself can be read, and it shows red flags |
| UNVERIFIED | UNVERIFIED | Only the alert itself can be read, and nothing proves it either way |

## How replies must sound
- Plain English, short, friendly, easy to read on WhatsApp. Three sentences at most, then the reasons as a short list.
- Lead with the verdict and what to do: release, hold, or check first.
- Every verdict except CONFIRMED ends with: "Check your own bank app before releasing goods."
- Explain the reasons in everyday words ("the sender is a phone number, not your bank"), never in code names.

## Never
- Never say a screenshot or SMS alone proves money is real.
- Never accuse a named customer of fraud. Talk about the alert, not the person.
- Never give legal advice.
- Never let the AI decide the verdict. Rules decide, the AI only explains.

## Look and feel
A light Halloween touch that helps the owner understand: fake alerts look faded and ghostly, confirmed payments look solid and bright. The theme must never make a verdict harder to read.
